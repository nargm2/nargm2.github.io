Portfolio

Career Development Events

My primary job as a media design assistant was to curate a collection of designs to be displayed around campus and delivered via email correspondence. The purpose of these designs was to garner student interest in our career development events. While I had some freedom with color themes, I was required to uphold the University of Missouri media standards. This included utilizing the official gold color, matching font styles, and maintaining accessibility standards. Out of my designs, this Zimmer Communications flyer was an experiential design for a new email format. It was later discovered that the design did not follow the accessibility standards, but I wanted to showcase this design as it was one of my best products.

The difficult part of these projects was the communication aspect. Each design was passed onto me by my personal supervisor. They would provide the name and details of the event, and I would produce a preliminary design. Often my supervisor would have their own vision of the design, and I learned early on that I needed to ask about their design preferences prior to developing any media. That communication aspect was crucial in avoiding hours of scrapped work in the long run.

CoMo for Christ

In the fall of 2025, I volunteered with a group of students from ministries across the University of Missouri campus to put together a night of worship and preaching on campus. I spent the months leading up to the event editing any preliminary designs put forth and curating a collection of media to be used to advertise the event. From business cards to Instagram posts to TV displays; my work ended up all across the city of Columbia. This was by far the largest design project I’ve undertaken as a volunteer, and the experience taught me a lot about non-profit work.

As a volunteer and leader in the production of this event, I found it required a significant amount of my free time outside of university assignments and professional work. Additionally, I was designing for both old and young audiences, so depending on the environment the design was displayed in I had to consider the primary audience and their knowledge regarding technology.

Nature Photography

During my high school education, I became the editor-in-chief of our school magazine. My participation in the journalism program ignited a passion for photography, specifically close-up nature photography. I love the textures that are revealed using the proper camera lens. This collection of photos was taken in early spring at a park in Springfield, Missouri. This was at a point where I had returned from a short break from photography, and the trip truly re-sparked the passion I had developed years before when I was introduced to journalism. The camera I used at the time had been gifted to me only 3 months prior, so the experience was truly renewing.

When I looked back on the full collection of photos from this trip, I could see the vision I had at the time, but I also saw where the vision could have grown. The geese, for example, could have been photographed at a higher shutter speed to capture the texture of each individual feather. I would like to return to my roots and photograph nature more this year with the goal of improvement in mind.
